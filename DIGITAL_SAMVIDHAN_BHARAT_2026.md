# DIGITAL SAMVIDHAN OF BHARAT 2026
## The Digital Constitution of India

---

# PREAMBLE

WE, THE PEOPLE OF BHARAT, in the digital age and in the enduring spirit of our Constitution adopted on the twenty-sixth day of November, 1949, and brought into force on the twenty-sixth day of January, 1950,

SOLEMNLY RESOLVE to constitute Bharat into a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic in the digital realm and to secure to all its digital citizens:

JUSTICE, social, economic and digital, in the circulation of information, in access to the network, and in the protection of the person online;

LIBERTY of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship in cyberspace, and of digital association, assembly and publication;

EQUALITY of status and of opportunity in digital access, in algorithmic treatment, and in the enjoyment of the network, and to promote among them all FRATERNITY assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation in the digital age;

IN OUR ASSEMBLY this twenty-eighth day of October, two thousand twenty-six, do HEREBY ADOPT, ENACT AND GIVE TO OURSELVES THIS DIGITAL CONSTITUTION OF BHARAT.

---

# PART I
## THE DIGITAL UNION AND ITS TERRITORY

**Article 1. Name and territory of the Digital Union.**  
(1) Bharat, that is India, shall be a Union of digital territories.  
(2) The territory of the Digital Union shall comprise:  
(a) the territories of the States as recognised by the Constitution of India;  
(b) the Union territories; and  
(c) such other digital territories, networks, gateways, nodes, and critical information infrastructure as may be acquired or constituted by law under this Digital Samvidhan.

**Article 2. Digital territory and jurisdiction.**  
The Digital Union shall exercise sovereignty over all data generated within, transmitted through, or stored upon digital infrastructure located within the territory of India, and over all digital acts having substantial effect within India, wherever the actor or the infrastructure may be physically situated.

**Article 3. Digital public infrastructure as national asset.**  
All digital public infrastructure, including identity systems, payment rails, data exchanges, and sovereign clouds, shall be national assets held in trust for the people and shall not be alienated to foreign control without the authority of law and the approval of Parliament.

---

# PART II
## DIGITAL CITIZENSHIP AND DIGITAL IDENTITY

**Article 4. Digital citizenship.**  
Every citizen of India shall possess digital citizenship, entitling them to a sovereign digital identity, access to the public network, and protection of their digital person.

**Article 5. Sovereign digital identity.**  
(1) The State shall establish and maintain a sovereign digital identity system for every citizen, grounded in biometric and demographic verification, which shall be the sole authoritative digital person of the individual in dealings with the State and with regulated private entities.  
(2) No digital identity shall be issued, renewed, or revoked except by due process of law.  
(3) The raw biometric template shall never be stored in plaintext, never be shared across agencies except as authorised by law, and shall be protected by quantum-resistant cryptography.

**Article 6. Digital domicile and residence.**  
A digital citizen shall have a digital domicile and a digital residence, which shall determine jurisdiction for digital disputes, taxation of digital services, and application of data-protection obligations.

**Article 7. Portability of digital identity.**  
Every digital citizen shall have the right to port their verified digital identity across service providers and across State boundaries within the Union, subject to security clearances and authentication standards.

---

# PART III
## FUNDAMENTAL DIGITAL RIGHTS

**Article 8. Definition of the State for digital purposes.**  
In this Part, “the State” includes the Union Government, State Governments, local authorities, digital public infrastructure operators performing public functions, and private platforms exercising powers that affect fundamental digital rights.

**Article 9. Equality before the digital law.**  
The State shall not deny to any digital citizen equality before the law or equal protection of the laws within the territory of India in the digital sphere, including in access to network services, algorithmic treatment, and digital public services.

**Article 10. Freedom of speech and expression in cyberspace.**  
(1) Every digital citizen shall have the right to freedom of speech and expression in digital spaces, including the right to publish, transmit, and receive information through any digital medium.  
(2) Nothing in sub-article (1) shall affect the operation of any existing law or prevent the State from making any law imposing reasonable restrictions on the exercise of the right conferred by sub-article (1) in the interests of the sovereignty and integrity of India, the security of the State, friendly relations with foreign States, public order, decency or morality, or in relation to contempt of court, defamation or incitement to an offence.  
(3) Any restriction under this Article shall be precise, narrow, proportionate, necessary in a democratic society, and subject to judicial review. Vague or overbroad restrictions shall be void.  
(4) The State shall not compel any platform to remove, shadow-ban, de-boost, or de-platform lawful speech except by a reasoned order of a competent authority, subject to appeal.

**Article 11. Right to privacy.**  
(1) Every person shall have the right to privacy, including digital privacy, as a fundamental right.  
(2) The right to privacy includes:  
(a) the right to bodily and mental integrity in the digital context;  
(b) the right to informational self-determination;  
(c) the right to confidentiality of communications;  
(d) the right to autonomy over personal data; and  
(e) the right to be free from unreasonable surveillance.  
(3) No person shall be subjected to arbitrary or unlawful interference with their privacy, family, home, correspondence, or digital communications.  
(4) Surveillance by the State shall be conducted only under the authority of law, upon reasonable grounds, with prior authorisation by a competent judicial or quasi-judicial authority, and shall be proportionate, time-bound, and subject to independent oversight and judicial review.

**Article 12. Right to digital dignity.**  
Every person shall have the right to digital dignity, which includes protection from doxxing, non-consensual intimate imagery, deepfake defamation, synthetic media impersonation, digital stalking, and algorithmic humiliation. The State shall enact and enforce law to protect digital dignity and shall provide swift remedies for violations.

**Article 13. Right to be forgotten under controlled conditions.**  
(1) A digital citizen may request the delinking, de-indexing, or erasure of personal data that is inaccurate, irrelevant, excessive, or no longer necessary for the purpose for which it was collected.  
(2) The right shall be balanced against the public interest, including archival, journalistic, research, and evidentiary needs.  
(3) The State shall establish a Digital Forgetting Tribunal to adjudicate requests, with appeal to the appropriate court.

**Article 14. Right against algorithmic discrimination.**  
(1) No algorithmic system, including artificial intelligence, machine learning models, scoring engines, and automated decision-making tools, shall discriminate against any person on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth, disability, language, or any other ground prohibited by the Constitution of India.  
(2) Every algorithmic system deployed by the State or by a significant data fiduciary shall be auditable, explainable, and subject to bias testing before deployment and periodically thereafter.  
(3) Decisions significantly affecting rights, liberties, or entitlements shall not be made solely by automated processing without meaningful human review.

**Article 15. Right to digital access.**  
(1) Every digital citizen shall have the right to affordable, non-discriminatory access to the public digital network and to essential digital public services.  
(2) The State shall endeavour to provide universal digital access, including in rural, tribal, and remote areas, and shall not impose unreasonable barriers to connectivity.

**Article 16. Right to digital property.**  
Every person shall have the right to own, control, and dispose of their digital assets, including data they have generated, digital tokens, and cryptographic keys, subject to law and the public interest.

**Article 17. Protection against self-incrimination in digital evidence.**  
No person accused of a digital offence shall be compelled to disclose their cryptographic keys, passwords, or authentication factors in a manner that amounts to compulsion to be a witness against themselves, except as provided by law and subject to judicial safeguards.

**Article 18. Laws inconsistent with Fundamental Digital Rights.**  
(1) All laws in force in the territory of India immediately before the commencement of this Digital Samvidhan, in so far as they are inconsistent with the provisions of this Part, shall, to the extent of such inconsistency, be void.  
(2) The State shall not make any law which takes away or abridges the rights conferred by this Part, and any law made in contravention of this clause shall, to the extent of the contravention, be void.

**Article 19. Enforcement of Fundamental Digital Rights.**  
The Supreme Court and the High Courts shall have the power to issue directions, orders, or writs for the enforcement of the rights conferred by this Part, including against digital platforms and intermediaries operating within India.

**Article 20. Non-derogability of certain digital rights.**  
Notwithstanding anything contained in this Digital Samvidhan, the rights conferred by Article 11 (Right to Privacy), Article 12 (Right to Digital Dignity), Article 14 (Right against Algorithmic Discrimination), and Article 17 (Protection against Self-Incrimination in Digital Evidence) shall not be suspended even during a Digital Emergency, except as expressly provided under Article 75.

# PART IV
## DIRECTIVE PRINCIPLES OF DIGITAL STATE POLICY

**Article 21. Application of the principles contained in this Part.**  
The provisions contained in this Part shall not be enforceable by any court, but the principles laid down therein are nevertheless fundamental in the governance of the Digital Union and it shall be the duty of the State to apply these principles in making laws and in governing digital affairs.

**Article 22. Digital public infrastructure for all.**  
The State shall strive to build and maintain sovereign digital public infrastructure, including identity, payments, data exchange, and cloud systems, that is accessible, interoperable, and secure.

**Article 23. Data minimisation and purpose limitation.**  
The State and every data fiduciary shall collect only the personal data necessary for a lawful purpose, shall process it only for that purpose, and shall not retain it longer than necessary.

**Article 24. Localisation of sovereign data.**  
The State shall endeavour to ensure that critical personal data and sovereign data are stored and processed within the territory of India, subject to exceptions provided by law for cross-border flows in the national interest.

**Article 25. Digital literacy and inclusion.**  
The State shall promote digital literacy among all citizens, with special attention to women, children, the elderly, persons with disabilities, and persons belonging to Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, and other backward classes.

**Article 26. Ethical artificial intelligence.**  
The State shall promote the development and deployment of artificial intelligence that is safe, transparent, accountable, and aligned with constitutional morality and the public interest.

**Article 27. Sustainable digital economy.**  
The State shall promote a sustainable digital economy that balances innovation with the protection of labour, environment, and public welfare.

---

# PART IVA
## FUNDAMENTAL DIGITAL DUTIES OF CITIZENS AND PLATFORMS

**Article 28. Fundamental digital duties.**  
It shall be the duty of every digital citizen and every digital platform operating in India:  
(a) to abide by this Digital Samvidhan and respect its ideals and institutions;  
(b) to refrain from spreading false or misleading information likely to cause public disorder, communal disharmony, or threats to national security;  
(c) to protect the sovereignty, unity, and integrity of India in cyberspace;  
(d) to respect the privacy and digital dignity of others;  
(e) to secure their own digital devices and credentials and not to use them for unlawful purposes;  
(f) to report cyber crimes and digital threats to the appropriate authorities;  
(g) to refrain from developing, deploying, or using artificial intelligence or automated systems that discriminate, deceive, or harm; and  
(h) to cooperate with lawful digital investigations and with constitutional authorities.

**Article 29. Duties of significant data fiduciaries and platforms.**  
(1) Every significant data fiduciary and intermediary shall appoint a Digital Compliance Officer resident in India.  
(2) They shall maintain records of content moderation, algorithmic promotion, and data access, and shall make such records available to constitutional authorities upon lawful demand.  
(3) They shall deploy human-reviewed grievance redressal mechanisms accessible to Indian users.

---

# PART V
## THE UNION DIGITAL EXECUTIVE, DIGITAL LEGISLATURE, AND DIGITAL JUDICIARY

**Article 30. The Union Digital Executive.**  
The executive power of the Digital Union shall be vested in the President and shall be exercised by the President directly or through officers subordinate to the President, in accordance with this Digital Samvidhan and the Constitution of India.

**Article 31. The Digital Sovereignty Council.**  
(1) There shall be a Digital Sovereignty Council comprising the Minister in charge of digital affairs, the Minister of Home Affairs, the Minister of External Affairs, the Minister of Electronics and Information Technology, the National Cyber Security Coordinator, and such other members as may be prescribed by law.  
(2) The Council shall advise the Union on matters of national digital security, cyber defence, data sovereignty, cross-border data flows, and digital emergencies.  
(3) The Council shall meet at least once every quarter and shall submit an annual report to Parliament.

**Article 32. The Digital Legislature.**  
Parliament shall have the power to make laws for the Digital Union with respect to any of the matters enumerated in the Seventh Schedule, including cyber security, data protection, artificial intelligence, critical information infrastructure, and digital identity.

**Article 33. The Digital Judiciary.**  
(1) There shall be a Supreme Court of India and such High Courts as may be constituted by law, which shall have jurisdiction over digital matters arising under this Digital Samvidhan.  
(2) The State shall establish specialised Digital Benches within the High Courts and a Digital Chamber within the Supreme Court for the expeditious disposal of digital disputes, cyber crimes, data-protection appeals, and constitutional challenges to digital governance.  
(3) Digital Courts shall function with technical assessors who possess expertise in cyber security, digital forensics, and data science.

---

# PART VI
## STATE DIGITAL GOVERNANCE

**Article 34. State Digital Executive.**  
The executive power of each State shall extend to digital governance within the State, subject to the provisions of this Digital Samvidhan and laws made by Parliament.

**Article 35. State Digital Data Authority.**  
Each State shall establish a State Digital Data Authority to oversee data governance, protect digital rights within the State, and coordinate with the National Digital Data Authority.

**Article 36. Local digital governance.**  
Panchayats and Municipalities shall promote digital inclusion, maintain local digital records, and deliver digital services to citizens, subject to standards prescribed by the State and the Union.

---

# PART VII
## CYBER CRIMES AND DIGITAL OFFENCES

**Article 37. Definition of cyber crime.**  
For the purposes of this Part, “cyber crime” means any unlawful act in which a computer, computer system, computer network, digital device, or data is the target, the tool, or the repository of evidence.

**Article 38. Offences against digital confidentiality, integrity, and availability.**  
(1) Unauthorised access to a computer system or network shall be an offence.  
(2) Unauthorised interception of data, unauthorised data modification, and unauthorised denial of service shall be offences.  
(3) The introduction, creation, or distribution of malicious code, including ransomware, worms, and logic bombs, shall be an offence.  
(4) Punishments shall be deterrent yet proportionate, graded according to the harm caused, the value affected, and whether the offence was committed for gain, malice, or on behalf of a foreign power.

**Article 39. Offences against digital identity and personal data.**  
(1) Identity theft, digital impersonation, and unauthorised use of another person’s digital identity shall be offences.  
(2) Unauthorised collection, disclosure, or sale of personal data, including biometric and health data, shall be an offence.  
(3) Doxxing and the non-consensual publication of personal identifying information shall be an offence.

**Article 40. Offences against digital dignity and vulnerable persons.**  
(1) Non-consensual intimate imagery, deepfake defamation, synthetic media impersonation, digital stalking, and cyber-bullying shall be offences.  
(2) Offences against children, women, persons with disabilities, and members of Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes in cyberspace shall carry enhanced punishment.  
(3) The online sexual exploitation of children and the distribution of child sexual abuse material shall be grave offences punishable with rigorous imprisonment.

**Article 41. Offences against the State and national digital security.**  
(1) Cyber espionage, cyber terrorism, and digital warfare against the State shall be grave offences.  
(2) Unauthorised access to, or interference with, critical information infrastructure shall be an offence punishable with imprisonment for life or with death, where the offence endangers national security or public safety.  
(3) Assisting a foreign power or foreign entity in committing cyber offences against India shall be treated as an offence against the sovereignty and integrity of India.

**Article 42. Offences against financial systems.**  
(1) Digital financial fraud, unauthorised electronic fund transfer, payment-system manipulation, and attacks on financial infrastructure shall be offences.  
(2) The use of digital assets or cryptocurrencies to evade lawful regulation, to finance terrorism, or to launder proceeds of cyber crime shall be an offence.

**Article 43. Offences involving artificial intelligence and synthetic media.**  
(1) The creation or distribution of synthetic media, including deepfakes, intended to deceive, defame, manipulate elections, or incite violence shall be an offence.  
(2) The deployment of autonomous systems or artificial intelligence that causes harm without human oversight, accountability, or explanation shall be an offence.  
(3) The circumvention of safety alignments, watermarking, or disclosure requirements for AI-generated content shall be an offence.

**Article 44. Jurisdiction and investigation.**  
(1) Cyber crimes shall be investigated by designated cyber-crime units and digital forensic laboratories established by the Union and the States.  
(2) Evidence collected digitally shall be admissible if it satisfies standards of authenticity, integrity, and chain of custody, and if it is hash-chained and tamper-evident.  
(3) International cooperation in cyber-crime investigation shall be conducted through mutual legal assistance treaties and sovereign channels, subject to constitutional safeguards.

# PART VIII
## NATIONAL DIGITAL SECURITY AND SOVEREIGN CYBER DEFENCE

**Article 45. Digital infrastructure as critical national infrastructure.**  
The internet, telecommunications networks, digital public infrastructure, financial systems, energy grids, health systems, and defence networks located within or serving India shall be treated as critical national infrastructure.

**Article 46. National Digital Security Agency.**  
(1) There shall be a National Digital Security Agency responsible for the defence of India’s digital territory, coordination of cyber threat intelligence, and response to cyber incidents.  
(2) The Agency shall operate under civilian oversight and shall be accountable to Parliament and to the Digital Sovereignty Council.  
(3) The Agency shall not engage in mass surveillance or the indiscriminate collection of communications content.

**Article 47. Sovereign cyber defence.**  
(1) The Union shall maintain offensive and defensive cyber capabilities solely for the defence of national sovereignty, territorial integrity, and constitutional order.  
(2) Any offensive cyber operation shall be authorised by the competent constitutional authority and shall be proportionate, necessary, and subject to after-action review by Parliament’s oversight committee.  
(3) The State shall defend against state-sponsored cyber warfare, information warfare, hybrid threats, and digital colonialism.

**Article 48. Critical information infrastructure protection.**  
(1) The Central Government shall designate critical information infrastructure and shall prescribe standards for its protection, including encryption, authentication, redundancy, and incident reporting.  
(2) Operators of critical information infrastructure shall report cyber incidents to the National Digital Security Agency within the time prescribed by law.  
(3) The State may take control of, or issue binding directions to, critical information infrastructure during a Digital Emergency under Article 75.

**Article 49. Quantum readiness.**  
The State shall endeavour to migrate critical infrastructure and sovereign communications to quantum-resistant cryptography and shall establish quantum-security standards for digital infrastructure.

---

# PART IX
## DATA SOVEREIGNTY AND CROSS-BORDER DATA FLOWS

**Article 50. Data sovereignty.**  
Data generated within India, or relating to persons in India, shall be subject to the sovereignty of India. The State shall regulate the collection, processing, storage, and transfer of such data.

**Article 51. Cross-border data flows.**  
(1) Personal data and sovereign data shall not be transferred outside the territory of India except:  
(a) to a country or organisation that provides an adequate level of data protection as determined by the National Digital Data Authority;  
(b) under standard contractual clauses or binding corporate rules approved by the Authority; or  
(c) in the national interest, as authorised by the Union Government.  
(2) The Authority shall maintain a list of countries and organisations providing adequate protection, which shall be reviewed periodically.  
(3) No transfer shall be permitted to a jurisdiction that does not respect the fundamental digital rights recognised by this Digital Samvidhan.

**Article 52. National Digital Data Authority.**  
(1) There shall be a National Digital Data Authority, an independent body responsible for data governance, data protection, and the supervision of data fiduciaries.  
(2) The Authority shall be headed by a Chairperson and such other members as may be appointed by the President on the recommendation of a selection committee comprising the Prime Minister, the Leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha, and the Chief Justice of India.  
(3) The Authority shall have powers to inquire, to direct, to impose penalties, and to enforce compliance with data-protection obligations.

**Article 53. Consent and lawful basis.**  
(1) Personal data shall be processed only with the free, informed, specific, and unambiguous consent of the data principal, or on another lawful basis prescribed by law.  
(2) Consent may be withdrawn at any time, and withdrawal shall not affect the lawfulness of processing based on consent before its withdrawal.  
(3) Processing of sensitive personal data, including biometric, health, genetic, and sexual-orientation data, shall require explicit consent and shall be subject to higher safeguards.

---

# PART X
## ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE, AUTONOMOUS SYSTEMS, AND ALGORITHMIC ACCOUNTABILITY

**Article 54. Algorithmic accountability.**  
(1) Every algorithmic system that significantly affects rights, liberties, entitlements, or access to essential services shall be accountable to a human authority.  
(2) The developer and the deployer of an algorithmic system shall be jointly and severally responsible for harms caused by the system, subject to the defence of due diligence.  
(3) Algorithmic systems used by the State shall be open to inspection, audit, and challenge by affected persons.

**Article 55. Transparency and explainability.**  
(1) Any person subject to a significantly adverse decision made by an algorithmic system shall have the right to an explanation of the decision, including the logic, significance, and consequences of the processing.  
(2) Significant data fiduciaries shall publish algorithmic impact assessments for systems that affect large numbers of persons.

**Article 56. Prohibited artificial intelligence.**  
The State shall prohibit:  
(a) artificial intelligence systems that assign social scores to citizens for the purpose of state control;  
(b) real-time remote biometric identification in publicly accessible spaces for law-enforcement purposes, except under strict judicial authorisation and for a limited purpose;  
(c) artificial intelligence that manipulates human behaviour through subliminal techniques or that exploits vulnerabilities of children or persons with disabilities; and  
(d) autonomous weapons systems that select and engage targets without meaningful human control.

**Article 57. Synthetic media and deepfakes.**  
(1) Synthetic media, including deepfakes, shall be identifiable through watermarking, provenance labelling, or disclosure, as prescribed by the National Digital Data Authority.  
(2) The deliberate creation or distribution of unlabelled synthetic media intended to deceive shall be an offence under Article 43.

---

# PART XI
## PROTECTION OF CRITICAL INFORMATION INFRASTRUCTURE

**Article 58. Designation and protection.**  
The Central Government shall designate systems and assets as critical information infrastructure and shall prescribe mandatory security standards, including continuous monitoring, incident reporting, red-team testing, and business-continuity planning.

**Article 59. Sectoral regulators.**  
Sectoral regulators for banking, telecommunications, energy, health, transport, and defence shall enforce critical-information-infrastructure protection standards within their sectors, in coordination with the National Digital Security Agency.

**Article 60. Incident response.**  
The Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In) and sectoral response teams shall serve as the national agencies for incident response, coordination, and public advisories during cyber incidents affecting critical infrastructure.

---

# PART XII
## DIGITAL ELECTION INTEGRITY

**Article 61. Integrity of digital elections.**  
The Election Commission of India shall ensure the integrity, security, and transparency of digital voting, electronic voting machines, voter-registration databases, and election-result transmission systems.

**Article 62. Protection against digital interference.**  
(1) No foreign power, foreign platform, or foreign-controlled algorithmic system shall intervene in Indian elections through digital means.  
(2) Political advertising on digital platforms shall be transparent, with disclosure of sponsors, expenditure, and targeting criteria.  
(3) Synthetic media used in election campaigns shall be labelled, and deceptive synthetic media intended to influence electoral outcomes shall be an offence.

---

# PART XIII
## RIGHTS OF CHILDREN, WOMEN, AND VULNERABLE GROUPS IN CYBERSPACE

**Article 63. Children in cyberspace.**  
(1) Every child shall have the right to protection from online exploitation, grooming, sexual abuse material, and harmful content.  
(2) The age of digital consent shall be prescribed by law, and processing of children’s personal data shall require parental or guardian consent and shall be subject to heightened safeguards.  
(3) The State shall establish special courts and fast-track mechanisms for offences against children in cyberspace.

**Article 64. Women in cyberspace.**  
(1) Every woman shall have the right to protection from gender-based cyber violence, including cyber-stalking, non-consensual intimate imagery, and online harassment.  
(2) The State shall provide specialised support, helplines, and fast-track courts for women facing cyber crimes.

**Article 65. Vulnerable groups.**  
Persons with disabilities, older persons, and members of Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, and other backward classes shall have the right to accessible digital infrastructure and protection from digital discrimination and hate speech.

# PART XV
## AMENDMENT, DIGITAL EMERGENCY, AND FINAL PROVISIONS

**Article 66. Amendment of this Digital Samvidhan.**  
(1) Parliament may, by law, amend any provision of this Digital Samvidhan by passing a bill in each House by a majority of the total membership of that House and by a majority of not less than two-thirds of the members of that House present and voting.  
(2) No amendment shall alter the basic structure of this Digital Samvidhan, including the sovereignty of India in the digital realm, the fundamental digital rights in Part III, the separation of powers, and judicial review.

**Article 67. Digital Emergency.**  
(1) If the President is satisfied that a grave digital emergency exists whereby the security of India, or any part of the territory thereof, is threatened by cyber war, large-scale cyber attack, or digital subversion, the President may proclaim a Digital Emergency.  
(2) Every proclamation shall be laid before each House of Parliament and shall cease to operate at the expiration of one month unless approved by resolution of both Houses.  
(3) During a Digital Emergency, the State may take measures necessary for the defence of digital infrastructure, including the direction of critical information infrastructure and the temporary suspension of certain digital services, subject to Article 20 and to judicial review.  
(4) No Digital Emergency shall continue for more than six months unless extended by Parliament.

**Article 68. Savings.**  
All laws in force in the territory of India immediately before the commencement of this Digital Samvidhan shall continue in force until altered, repealed, or amended by a competent authority, subject to the provisions of this Digital Samvidhan.

**Article 69. Oath of digital office.**  
Every person appointed to a digital office under this Digital Samvidhan shall make and subscribe an oath or affirmation in the form set out in the Third Schedule.

**Article 70. Power to remove difficulties.**  
If any difficulty arises in giving effect to the provisions of this Digital Samvidhan, the President may, by order, make such provisions as appear necessary for the removal of the difficulty, provided that no such order shall be made after the expiration of three years from the commencement of this Digital Samvidhan.

**Article 71. Short title and commencement.**  
(1) This Digital Samvidhan may be cited as the Digital Constitution of India, 2026 (Digital Samvidhan of Bharat, 2026).  
(2) It shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification, appoint.

---

**END OF THE DIGITAL SAMVIDHAN OF BHARAT 2026**

---

*This instrument is a foundational digital constitution for the Republic of India, designed to operate in concert with the Constitution of India adopted on 26 November 1949. It confers no power on any software project, developer, or private entity to override constitutional authority, human review, or judicial sovereignty. All governance under this Digital Samvidhan remains subject to human deliberation, constitutional morality, and the rule of law.*
